# Step 7 — Product Critic review of the complete dashboard

Scope: `dashboard/index.html` (built page) and `dashboard/template.html`, judged in headless Chromium at 1440×900, 1280×800 and 400 px wide, on all four run tabs. Scripts, the recolored copy and screenshots are in `reviews/step7_product_critic/` (`probe.py`, `table.py`, `black.py`, `index_recolored.html`, `fold_*.png`, `full_*.png`, `recolor_*_full.png`).

What passed, checked rather than assumed:
- **Collapsed marks:** no mark with a value above 0 renders narrower or shorter than 2 px, on any run, at any of the three widths (`probe.py`: `collapsed: []` on all 12 run×width combinations).
- **No horizontal page scroll:** `scrollWidth` equals the viewport width at 1440, 1280 and 400.
- **Filters and live count:** my own counts from `runs/*/predictions.jsonl` match both `#shown-count` and the number of visible rows in every case I tried. Examples on balanced150_v3: mistakes 40, Neutral→Negative 25, Negative→Neutral 4. Clicking a heatmap cell sets the filters correctly (Positive→Neutral gives 2). Filters reset when switching runs.
- **Recoloring:** it works from the one `:root` block. I replaced all 17 color tokens in a copy with a dark theme and garish class and right/wrong colors. Every visible color on the page changed, across all four runs. The only colors that stayed `rgb(0, 0, 0)` belong to non-rendered elements: HTML, HEAD, META, TITLE, STYLE. The recolored page stays coherent (`recolor_recolored_full.png`).
- **Look:** palette, type scale and card layout are deliberate. Nothing looks like a default chart library.

Findings, most severe first:

MAJOR — `dashboard/template.html` `renderReviews` / `.table-wrap` and `td.text { min-width: 300px }` (Review column of the review table, default run) — On a laptop screen, the review text sits almost entirely off-screen inside the table's horizontal scroller. The table's own claim is "enough per-review detail to back up what you claim", but a reader sees IDs, chips and a clamped title, with no cue that the text column exists. On the default balanced run, only 33 px of the 300 px Review column is visible at both laptop sizes. — `.venv/bin/python reviews/step7_product_critic/table.py` → `1440 balanced150_v3 {'wrapClient': 1056, 'tableScroll': 1324, 'reviewColLeft': 1025, 'reviewColWidth': 300, 'visibleOfReview': 33}`, the same at 1280. first100_v3 and first100_v2 show 203 of 300 px. In `full_balanced150_v3_1440.png` the header is cut to "Rev" and the text to "I ha…". — Fit the table to the 1056 px card instead of scrolling it. Options: drop the separate ID column into a small subline; merge "Answer key / Model / Result" into one cell (for example "Neutral → Negative ✕"); merge "Model emotion / Word list" into one cell; let Title and Review share one column (bold title above the clamped text). If horizontal scroll must stay, put Title and Review immediately after Result, so the text is visible before the emotion columns.

MAJOR — `dashboard/template.html` `renderConfusion` (`strength > 55 ? " dark"`) and `.cell.dark { color: var(--on-fill) }` — White text on light amber heatmap cells is close to unreadable. It hits the biggest mistake on the page, Neutral→Negative 25 (50.0% of row): the one number the balanced run exists to show. The light/dark text switch uses color-mix strength only, and amber at the same strength is much lighter than slate. — `black.py` reads the cell as `color(srgb 0.915 0.717 0.514)` with text `rgb(255, 255, 255)`. WCAG contrast computed from those values: `white on N->Neg 25 cell 1.82`. It is `1.8` at 56% amber and still only `2.93` at 100% amber. The same rule gives white on ochre in the emotion cross-tab (Tie column, 25 and 24). — Pick the text color from the fill's luminance, not from the mix percentage. Or use separate thresholds per tone, or simply keep `--ink` on every `--miss`/`--lex` cell and `--on-fill` only on `--hit` cells. Add a contrast assertion (at least 4.5:1) for heatmap text to `verify_dashboard.py`.

MINOR — `dashboard/template.html` copy in `renderConfusion` ("Slate cells on the diagonal are right; amber cells are mistakes") and `renderEmotion` ("Slate diagonal = same emotion; ochre = different") — The theme can be recolored from `:root`, but these sentences hard-code color names, so any recolor makes the explanations false. — In `index_recolored.html`, right cells render mint and wrong cells magenta (`recolor_recolored_full.png`), while the text still says "Slate … amber" and "ochre". — Refer to the legend keys ("diagonal = right, see key") or drop the color words. The legends already show swatches from the tokens.

MINOR — `dashboard/template.html` `renderBlurb`, fed by `run_meta.json#selection_rule` (first text under the tabs) — The first sentence a non-technical reader meets is Python: "150 reviews: for each three-class truth label, random.Random(42).sample(sorted included review_ids of that class, 50); ids saved to sample_ids.json." It sits above the verdict and pushes the hero down, to y=331 at 1440×900. At 400 px the blurb plus tabs push the verdict sentence to y=739, below the first phone screen. — `probe.py` fold positions for balanced150_v3 at 1440: `'blurb': [183, 267], 'hero': [331, 399]`, and at 400: `'verdictHead': [739, 852]`; the text is visible in `fold_balanced150_v3_1440.png`. — Show a plain-language line in the blurb, for example "150 reviews: 50 drawn at random from each class (fixed seed 42)". Leave the exact rule where it already appears, in "How this run was made".

MINOR — First screen at 1440×900 and 1280×800 (`#sec-verdict` followed by `#sec-describe`) — The verdict is clear in numbers ("73.3% … any one class would score 33.3% … Weakest class: Neutral, 17 of 50 right"), but no chart of the failure appears on the first screen. The paired Stars-vs-Model bars (Neutral 50 vs 23) start just under the fold; the heatmap and the right/wrong bars start at about y=1155. The run's real story, that 3★ reviews mostly collapse into Negative (25 of 50), is not on the first screen. — `probe.py`: `'describe': [666, 1131], 'confusion': [1155, 1619]` at both laptop sizes; `fold_balanced150_v3_1440.png` shows only the star-chart headers at the bottom edge. — Add the per-class right/wrong bar (three rows, already built in `renderClasses`) to the verdict card in place of the text-only answer-key line. Or name the dominant confusion in the verdict line, bound to `confusion_cells`, for example "Neutral reviews most often go to: Negative, 25 of 50".

MINOR — `dashboard/template.html` `renderConfusion` / `renderDescribe` (UNPARSED column and row) — On every run, the heatmap spends a quarter of its width on an Unparsed column of zeros. The paired-bar chart adds an Unparsed row reading "Stars — not a star class / Model 0". This is clutter that thins the heatmap cells on a laptop screen. — `metrics.json` `prediction_distribution.counts.UNPARSED` is 0 for balanced150_v3, and the probe's `{'pred': 'UNPARSED'}` filter shows 0 on all four runs. `full_balanced150_v3_1440.png` shows the all-zero fourth column. — Render the Unparsed column, row and filter option only when the count is above 0. The hero line "Unreadable model replies: 0" already reports the zero.

MINOR — `dashboard/template.html` `.run-tabs` at 400 px — On a phone the four run tabs wrap into a vertical stack about 190 px tall. With the eyebrow and blurb, the hero number starts at y=589, so the first phone screen is mostly navigation and method text. — `fold_balanced150_v3_400.png`; `probe.py` 400 px `'hero': [589, 641]`. — Under 560 px, make the tabs a single horizontally scrolling row (`flex-wrap: nowrap; overflow-x: auto`) or a `<select>`, and shorten the blurb as in the finding above.

MINOR — `dashboard/template.html` `:root` (no `color-scheme` token) — A dark recolor from the one block leaves browser-drawn parts light: select dropdown lists, the search field's clear button and scrollbars. That breaks the "recolor by editing one block" promise for dark themes. — `probe.py` sample on the recolored copy: `'colorScheme': 'normal'` while `body` is `rgb(15, 23, 32)`. — Add `color-scheme: light;` to the `:root` block, with a comment that it should become `dark` when the palette is dark.
