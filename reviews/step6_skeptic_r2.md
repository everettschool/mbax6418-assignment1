# Step 6 — Skeptic review, round 2

Scripts and outputs: `reviews/step6_skeptic_r2/checks.py` → `checks_out.txt`; `dashboard_text2.py` → `dashboard_<run>.txt` (headless Chromium, runs switched by clicking `[data-run-tab]`, every `<tbody>` removed). I made no model calls. No other seed was classified, so the robustness points below cover only what goes into a sample, not how the model would score it.

What held up:
- The confusion cells, recalls, precisions, Wilson intervals, re-weighted figures, and the emotion counts (16/150, 16/59, 67 ties, 24 none, best constant anticipation 22) match the saved files.
- first100 v1→v3: only 46 and 83 changed, as stated.
- The re-weighting supports "the accuracy fall is the class mix". Applying the balanced run's per-class recalls to batch_100's 93/2/5 mix gives about 94.5%, close to the observed 95.0%.
- Rating leak: the rating is not in the cache key (`cache_key(model, prompt_text, title, text)`). The retry resends the same `messages`. Only 121389 and 140676 contain star wording in the sent content, even with a broader regex (`checks_out.txt` §B).

MAJOR — `reviews/step6_analysis.md` l.80 ("Most of the NEUTRAL→NEGATIVE cell is the star rating and the review's words disagreeing") and l.128 ("**So the finding is mostly about the answer key.**") — The reasoning is circular. "It follows the prompt's own NEGATIVE rules" shows that the model obeyed a policy the author wrote. It does not show that the stars are the wrong answer. A customer who had a card problem and still gave 3★ has, by their own rating, called it a middling experience. The v3 rules "could not use the card, lost money, or say they would not buy again → NEGATIVE" were carried over from v2 unchanged, while the lukewarm rule was deliberately moved toward the three-class key (ISSUES l.89). So the cell measures disagreement between the prompt's policy and the key, and that policy is a design choice. No independent human label supports "the key is wrong". — Evidence: `prompts/sentiment_v2.txt` l.10 already has "If they could not use the card, lost money, or say they would not buy again, pick NEGATIVE". ISSUES l.89 lists only the lukewarm and equal-weight changes as the v3 adjustments. The analysis cites the rules for 33885, 114708, 6689, 60608 and 53136 as reasons the rows are "not model failures". — Suggested fix: reframe as "the prompt's mixed-review policy (inherited from the binary prompt) sends card-problem reviews to NEGATIVE, while their authors rated them 3★". Say that whether the prompt or the key is "right" is not settled by this run. If it matters, name a prompt variant as untested future work. Drop "mostly about the answer key".

MINOR — `reviews/step6_analysis.md` l.84–88, l.165 (blanked titles) — An empty sent TITLE is not neutral information. No included row in the file has an originally empty title, so an empty title always means "this title was an auto-filled star phrase". That happens at different rates per class, and the prompt tells the model "Either the TITLE or the TEXT may be empty". The blank carries a weak, class-correlated signal derived from the rating field, and the NEUTRAL row shows blanked rows behaving differently. — Evidence: `checks_out.txt` §B2: `originally empty title 0 (0.0%)` in all three pools; blanked share POSITIVE 22.7%, NEUTRAL 13.1%, NEGATIVE 5.8%. §E: blanked NEUTRAL rows 4/10 predicted POSITIVE vs 4/40 for the rest, Fisher one-sided p = 0.041. The texts themselves are short and positive ("good", "Children loved them"), so the words may explain these rows fully. — Suggested fix: add a caveat that an empty title only occurs after blanking, so its presence is weakly informative. Do not claim the blank carries no signal.
