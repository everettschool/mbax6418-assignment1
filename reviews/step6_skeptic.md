# Step 6 — Skeptic review

Scripts and saved outputs are in `reviews/step6_skeptic/`: `checks.py` → `checks_out.txt`, `nrc_words_out.txt`, `seed_titles_out.txt`, `dashboard_text.py` → `dashboard_<run>.txt`. I made no model calls. Samples drawn with other seeds were only inspected for their star mix and titles, not classified, so any claim below about another seed covers what went into the sample, not how the model would score it.

What held up: the confusion-matrix cells, recalls and Wilson figures match `metrics.json` and `comparisons.json`. The saved ids match a fresh draw. No field other than title and text reaches the model (`verify_leak.py` PASS; every stored `messages` equals `build_messages(title, text)`). The 25/8/4/2/1 id lists match `misclassified_review_ids`. The "topic words" reason for the word list's emotions does hold on the balanced run: in 17 of the 22 rows where the word list answers anticipation, "gift" is one of the words behind it (`nrc_words_out.txt`). The problems are below.

---

MAJOR — `reviews/step6_analysis.md` l.30–31, l.95–97, l.106; dashboard precision footnote (`template.html`: "When the model says a class, how often it is right (precision): …") and the balanced-tab headline — Precision and accuracy measured on the balanced sample are presented as facts about the model. They are not: they depend on the 1:1:1 class mix the sample imposes. "When it does say NEUTRAL it is usually right (73.9% precision)", "25 of 70 NEGATIVE predictions are 3★" (framed as something the lopsided run "hid"), and "The balanced three-class run is the honest measure: 73.3%" would all change sharply if the same per-class behaviour were applied to the real class mix. The "+2.0 → +40.0 pts" margin comparison also sets margins over two different baselines side by side, as if the model had gained something. — Evidence: `checks.py` §5 takes the balanced run's per-row confusion shares and weights them by the file's class pools (POSITIVE 88.55%, NEUTRAL 2.15%, NEGATIVE 9.31%): `population-weighted accuracy: 0.9411`; `precision NEUTRAL: 0.1455 (balanced-sample precision 0.7391)`; `precision NEGATIVE: 0.8864 (balanced 0.6429)`; `share of NEGATIVE predictions that are 3★ (population-weighted): 0.1136 (balanced: 25/70 = 0.3571)`; `share of NEUTRAL predictions from 4-5★ (population-weighted): 0.7061`. So on real traffic, about 85% of NEUTRAL answers would be wrong, mostly on 4–5★ reviews. That conclusion is the reverse of "usually right". (It is also a rough estimate: it rests on 2/50 POSITIVE→NEUTRAL, and 4★ reviews are missing from the sample, see below.) — Suggested fix: state that recall and balanced accuracy are what the balanced sample measures well, and that precision and plain accuracy on it reflect the forced 1:1:1 mix. Drop "usually right" and "honest measure", or add the population-weighted figures computed from saved fields with a named field in `comparisons.json`. Add one sentence on the dashboard next to the precision footnote for balanced runs. Replace the margin comparison with balanced accuracy (65.6% vs 73.3%), which is at least measured the same way in both runs, and note that first100_v3's figure rests on 2 NEUTRAL and 5 NEGATIVE rows.

MAJOR — `reviews/step6_analysis.md` l.52–63 ("For these, the model weighed the complaint over the praise, against its prompt") and l.112 ("At least 7 of the 25 are mixed reviews the model tipped to NEGATIVE against its instructions") — The count of 7 comes from finding praise words in a review. It does not check what the prompt's own rules say about that review. For at least four of the seven, the v3 rules point to NEGATIVE, so the model followed its instructions. — Evidence (full sent text, `checks_out.txt` §4; rules quoted from `prompts/sentiment_v3.txt`):
- 88787: title "Still a happy recipient of this gift.", text "…took almost five days to be mailed to me. Very disappointed." The prompt says "Title and text disagree: the TEXT decides", so NEGATIVE.
- 60608: "…I'm disappointed I can't save every time… It's a brilliant idea but I probably won't be using it again." The prompt says "if they… say they would not buy again, pick NEGATIVE".
- 53136: "I didnt get my 10$ for reloding 100$. 😣". The promised $10 was not received, which is close to "lost money → NEGATIVE".
- 121389: "HOWEVER!! … I have NOT had ability to use Amex points… Bad bad bad". The complaint clearly outweighs "a breeze".

Only 32735, 60681 and 147336 are plausibly the "good and bad carry about equal weight → NEUTRAL" case, and even those are judgment calls. — Suggested fix: re-grade each of the seven against the prompt's specific rules. Report "up to 3 of 25" (or whatever the re-grade gives) as possible failures to follow instructions. Change l.112 and the "It is still a real weakness" bullet to match. The honest takeaway is closer to "most of the 25 are NEGATIVE by the prompt's own rules, so the 3★→NEGATIVE cell mostly measures disagreement between the stars and the words, not a model failure".

MINOR — Dashboard, `balanced150_v3` tab (`reviews/step6_skeptic/dashboard_balanced150_v3.txt`) — The page shows "Positive 50 reviews · 48 · 96.0% of row" and the precision footnote, but says nowhere that the balanced POSITIVE class has no 4★ reviews, or that balanced-sample precision is not a real-traffic figure. Those caveats exist only in the analysis file, so a non-technical reader of the page cannot see them. — Evidence: `grep -i -E "4★|no 4|four-star|population"` over the visible page text finds only the answer-key blurb "4–5★ positive". In `checks.py` §6, 63 of 1,000 seeds draw zero 4★ (`share with 0: 0.063`), so seed 42 is a 6% outcome that inflates POSITIVE recall on exactly the reviews most likely to sit near NEUTRAL. — Suggested fix: add a data-bound note to the balanced run's blurb, built from `sample_ids.json` / star counts: star mix 1★ 43, 2★ 7, 3★ 50, 4★ 0, 5★ 50.

MINOR — `reviews/step6_analysis.md` l.136 ("One review's own text mentions stars (121389)"); `ISSUES.md` Step 6 entry "A customer's own star mention reached the model" — Two balanced-run reviews sent a star count to the model, not one. The read-back scanned texts only, not titles. — Evidence: `checks.py` §1: `140676 1.0 NEGATIVE ['stars'] | TITLE: <<<I would give this 'zero stars'>>>`, and `near-miss star titles not blanked: [(140676, "I would give this 'zero stars'")]`. `verify_leak.py` itself reports "3 reviews use a forbidden word" (121389 "5 stars", 140676 "zero stars", 138151 "Helpful Review"). The analysis accounts for only one. The effect is small: 140676 is a correct NEGATIVE, and its text "card did not work" decides it anyway. — Suggested fix: list both rows (and name the third hit as not rating-related) in the analysis caveat and in ISSUES.md, and scan titles as well as texts in the read-back.

MINOR — `reviews/step6_analysis.md` l.114–121 ("Would another sample change the story?") and PROGRESS "Make your results repeatable" — The robustness section treats sampling error as the only source of variation. The run itself shows the endpoint is not deterministic at temperature 0, and the analysis never mentions the retry. — Evidence: `checks.py` §2: `4880 from_cache False ['LABEL=NEGATIVE;EMOTION=disappointment', 'LABEL=NEGATIVE;EMOTION=sadness']`. Identical messages at `temperature: 0` produced two different replies. The saved emotion for 4880 comes from the second one (`rows_needing_retry: 1` in `metrics.json`; the analysis l.14 reports only "Parse failures 0, API errors 0"). Repeatability of the published numbers therefore rests on `cache/`, not on the model. — Suggested fix: disclose the one retry and the differing reply. Say that re-running without the cache may change individual labels, and that the Wilson intervals do not cover this.

MINOR — `reviews/step6_analysis.md` l.118 and l.135; `ISSUES.md` Step 6 "Sample quirks" — Three problems:
- (a) "balanced accuracy of the run 65.7%–79.8% (`balanced150_v3_accuracy_wilson95`)" gives the Wilson interval of plain accuracy over 150 rows the name "balanced accuracy", and treats a stratified sample as a simple random one.
- (b) Seed 42 is unusual in a way that bears on one of the cells being interpreted. Four of the 8 3★→POSITIVE errors are reviews whose "Three Stars" title was blanked, and seed 42 drew 10 such titles into the NEUTRAL class, against a mean of 6.6 across seeds.
- (c) ISSUES.md explains the 26 blanked titles as "because the balanced draw reaches older reviews". That was never tested, and the pool shares give a simpler cause.

Evidence: `seed_titles_out.txt`: `seeds with >=10 'Three Stars' titles in NEUTRAL draw: 0.117 | mean 6.613`. `checks_out.txt` §6: blanked titles across the whole draw have median 21 (5–95%: 14–28), seed 42 has 26, and `pool share of star-phrase titles by class: {'NEGATIVE': 0.058, 'NEUTRAL': 0.1309, 'POSITIVE': 0.2269}`. The high count follows from about 23% of the POSITIVE pool having auto-filled titles (batch_100 is simply unusually low). §3: blanked NEUTRAL rows score 5/10 correct, unblanked 12/40.

Suggested fix: rename the interval "accuracy". Note that the 3★→POSITIVE count of 8 leans on an above-average number of blanked-title 3★ reviews (top ~12% of seeds). Replace the "older reviews" explanation with the pool-share numbers.

MINOR — `reviews/step6_analysis.md` l.131 ("The balanced sample shifts the LLM's emotions toward anger (62) and trust (22), as expected with 100 non-positive reviews") — The anger part holds. The trust part is not explained by the non-positive reviews. — Evidence: `checks.py` §7, LLM emotion by truth on balanced150_v3: trust comes from POSITIVE 10, NEUTRAL 11, NEGATIVE 1. Only 12 of 22 trust answers are on non-positive reviews, and trust on POSITIVE reviews rose to 10/50 (20%) from 9/93 (9.7%) on first100_v3. — Suggested fix: attribute the anger shift to the non-positive classes. Report the trust split by class instead of calling it expected.
